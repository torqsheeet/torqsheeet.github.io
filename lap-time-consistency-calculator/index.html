Lap time consistency calculator

Your best lap says how fast you can go once. Your average and your spread say how fast you actually go, lap after lap, which is what a race is made of. Paste the laps from one session and the calculator works out both, ignoring out laps and spins if you want it to.

% s
Runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. The example laps are made up.

What the numbers mean

  • Best to average gap: how far your typical lap is from your best one. If you shave your best lap but this gap grows, the race pace may not have improved at all.
  • Standard deviation: how much your laps scatter around the average, in seconds. Two drivers with the same average can have very different spreads; the smaller one makes fewer mistakes.
  • Laps close to your best: a plain count that is easy to compare between sessions, as long as you keep the same margin.
  • Best three-lap run: the fastest average over three laps in a row with none of them ignored. It is a fairer pace figure than one lucky lap.

The numbers are only comparable for the same track, car and conditions. When you test a setup change, change one thing at a time and run enough laps that one good or bad lap does not decide it.

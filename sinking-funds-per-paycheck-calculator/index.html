Sinking funds calculator: what to save from each paycheck

A sinking fund is money you put aside a little at a time for a cost you know is coming: the car insurance renewal, Christmas, a yearly subscription, the next set of tyres. The bill stops being a surprise because it was paid for in small pieces. The useful question is how much to move on each payday, and that depends on how many paydays you have before each due date. How the numbers work

For each goal: (amount minus what you have already saved) divided by the number of paydays from your next one up to the due date. A goal due soon gets a large share; a goal a year away gets a small one. The total is what to move on your next payday. When a goal is paid, its share drops out and the total falls, so the first few months are usually the most expensive. If that first total is too high, push back a due date you control (a holiday, a gadget), lower an amount, or start with the goals that would otherwise land on a credit card.

Spreading every cost evenly over the year instead (yearly total divided by paychecks per year: 52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 twice a month, 12 monthly) gives a smaller, flat amount, but only works if each fund already holds enough for the bills that fall early in the year.

Common sinking funds

  • Insurance paid yearly or every six months (car, home, contents).
  • Christmas and birthdays; see also how many paychecks until Christmas 2026.
  • Car upkeep: servicing, tyres, registration or road tax.
  • Yearly subscriptions and memberships.
  • Holidays and travel.
  • Medical, dental and glasses costs not covered elsewhere.

Biweekly pay means some months have three paydays; those are a good time to top up the funds that are behind. See three-paycheck months in 2027.

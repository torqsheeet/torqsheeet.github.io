Home maintenance fund calculator: what to save from each paycheck

Home repairs have no due date: the roof, the water heater, the fence, the dishwasher all fail when they fail. That makes them hard to budget for, so give the fund a yearly target instead of a deadline. A common rule of thumb is about 1% of the home's value a year (more for an older home or one in a harsh climate). If you have a few years of your own repair bills, their average is a better starting point. This calculator turns either into an amount per paycheck, and suggests a cap so the fund does not grow forever.


Runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. These are planning estimates, not a quote: a single repair (a roof, a foundation) can cost several years of the fund.

How the numbers work

Yearly target: home value times the share you picked, or the average of the repair bills you entered. Per paycheck: the yearly target divided by your paydays a year (52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 twice a month, 12 monthly). Example: a $300,000 home at 1% is $3,000 a year, which is $115.38 a biweekly paycheck or $250 a month.

The suggested cap is one year's target or your worst single year, whichever is larger. Once the fund reaches it, stop adding and send that money elsewhere; after a repair, refill it at the same rate. A year with several big repairs at once is what an emergency fund is for.

Other costs that work the same way

  • Car repairs and tyres: average your last few years, cap at your worst year.
  • Vet bills, dental work, glasses.
  • Replacing a phone or laptop every few years: its price divided by the years it lasts.

Costs with a known due date (insurance renewals, registration, Christmas) are easier: count the paydays to the date with the sinking funds calculator.

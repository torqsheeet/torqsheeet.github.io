27 paychecks in 2027?

Short answer: 2027 has 365 days, which is 52 weeks and one day, and that extra day is a Friday: the year starts on Friday January 1 and ends on Friday December 31. So:

Not sure which Friday pattern you are on? Count 14 days at a time from any payday you know, or use the checker on the three-paycheck months in 2027 page, which lists every biweekly pattern.

The January 1 catch

January 1 is New Year's Day, a bank holiday in the US and many other countries. Many payrolls pay on the last working day before a holiday payday, which would be Thursday 31 December 2026. If yours does, that paycheck lands in 2026, and 2027 has 26 paychecks after all (2026 then has 27). Others pay the next working day, which keeps it in 2027. Your payroll department or pay calendar is the only reliable answer for your job; the counts above use the plain calendar dates.

What a 27th paycheck does to each paycheck

If you are paid by the hour, a 27th payday just means another period of hours paid. For a fixed annual salary, employers generally take one of two approaches, and it is worth asking which:

  • Salary divided by 27: your yearly pay stays the same, so every paycheck in 2027 is a little smaller than in a 26-paycheck year.
  • Same paycheck as usual, 27 times: each paycheck stays the same and the year pays one extra paycheck.
Gross pay before tax and deductions. Runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Also check anything taken as a fixed amount per paycheck: a benefit premium or a retirement contribution set per paycheck to reach a yearly target is taken 27 times instead of 26, so the yearly total comes out about 4% higher than planned. If you set those amounts yourself, it may be worth recalculating them for 2027.

Budgeting a 27-paycheck year

On the Friday 1 January pattern, January, July and December each get three paychecks (1, 15, 29 Jan; 2, 16, 30 Jul; 3, 17, 31 Dec). A common approach is to budget every month on two paychecks so fixed bills never depend on a third, and give each third paycheck a job in advance: a sinking fund, an extra debt payment, or savings (see paychecks left until Christmas 2026 for one worked example). If your paychecks get smaller because the salary is divided by 27, the two-paycheck months get slightly tighter, so build the budget on the 2027 paycheck amount, not the 2026 one. This is general budgeting information, not financial, tax or payroll advice.

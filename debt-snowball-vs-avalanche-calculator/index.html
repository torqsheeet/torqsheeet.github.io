Debt snowball vs avalanche calculator

Two common ways to pay off several debts. Both pay every minimum each month and put everything extra on one target debt; when a debt is paid off, its minimum rolls over onto the next target. The snowball targets the smallest balance first. The avalanche targets the highest interest rate first. Enter your own numbers to see what the difference is for you.

How the numbers are worked out

Month by month: interest is added at APR / 12 on each balance, every minimum is paid (or the remaining balance, if smaller), then the extra plus any minimums freed by paid-off debts goes to the target debt, and if the target is cleared the rest moves to the next one. "Minimums only" pays the minimums and nothing else. Real lenders compute interest daily and minimums often shrink as balances fall, so treat the results as an estimate for comparing the two methods, not as a payoff quote. This is general budgeting information, not financial advice.

Which one to pick

On interest alone the avalanche usually costs less than the snowball (or the same), and the gap is often small when rates are close. The snowball clears whole debts sooner, which some people find easier to stick with. The calculator shows both, so you can see what that trade costs in your case.

50/30/20 budget calculator: split each paycheck

The 50/30/20 rule splits your take-home pay (after tax and deductions) three ways: about 50% for needs (rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport to work, minimum debt payments), 30% for wants (eating out, streaming, hobbies, trips) and 20% for savings and extra debt payments. Most budgets explain it per month, but you are paid per paycheck, so this calculator does the split per payday as well.


Runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. The split is a starting point, not a test you pass or fail: what matters is that something goes to savings every payday.

How the numbers work

Per paycheck: take-home pay times each share. Per month: the per-paycheck amount times your paydays a year, divided by 12 (52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 twice a month, 12 monthly). Example: $2,000 take-home every two weeks is $52,000 a year, or $4,333.33 a month. At 50/30/20 that is $1,000 for needs, $600 for wants and $400 for savings from each paycheck, or $2,166.67, $1,300 and $866.67 a month.

If you are paid every two weeks, most months have two paydays and two months a year have three. Budget your monthly bills on two paychecks, and the third paycheck in those months is free to go to savings or debt; see the three-paycheck months in 2027.

If your real needs come to more than half of your take-home pay, that is common where rent is high. Try 60/20/20 and keep the 20% savings, rather than cutting savings first.

Where the 20% can go

  • A starter emergency fund, first.
  • Bills that come once a year (insurance, registration, Christmas): a sinking fund per paycheck.
  • Debt above the minimum payment: compare snowball and avalanche.

This is general budgeting information, not financial advice.
